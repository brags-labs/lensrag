# Global HR Policy Manual — BragsLabs

This manual governs employment at **BragsLabs** ("the Company") in every work mode — on-site, hybrid, remote and off-site — across its operations in the **the United States, India, the United Kingdom, Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland, Singapore and Canada**. Chapters 1–35 set the Company's global standards, Chapter 36 covers cross-border work, Chapter 37 compares key entitlements, and Chapters 38–45 are country addenda with the local legal rules. Policy version 2.0, effective 1 January 2024, owned by the Chief Human Resources Officer, Anita Desai (chro@bragslabs.com). Superseded by version 3.0 on 1 November 2026.

*BragsLabs Global HR Policy Manual · version 2.0 · effective 1 January 2024. Superseded by version 3.0 on 1 November 2026.*

## Chapter 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This manual sets out the rules, entitlements and responsibilities that govern employment at BragsLabs and all its subsidiaries worldwide ("the Company"). It exists so that every employee, wherever and however they work, is treated by the same standards and knows what the Company expects of them.

The Company operates four work modes: on-site, hybrid, remote and off-site. Most policies apply equally to all four. Where a rule differs by work mode, the relevant chapter says so explicitly, and Chapter 2 explains how work modes are assigned.

The objectives of this manual are to:

- give employees a single, reliable reference for HR matters;
- help managers apply policies fairly and consistently across teams, locations and work modes;
- ensure compliance with the employment, social security, data protection and workplace safety laws of every country where the Company operates;
- support a productive, safe, inclusive and respectful workplace, whether physical or virtual;
- protect the Company's clients, information and reputation.

### 1.2 Scope and applicability

This manual applies to all employees on the payroll of the Company or any of its subsidiaries, in any country, including:

| Category | Description | Applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent employees | Employed for an indefinite period after confirmation | All chapters |
| Probationers | Permanent hires serving probation | All chapters, with probation-specific rules in Chapter 7 |
| Fixed-term employees | Employed for a defined period or project | All chapters, pro rata where stated |
| Trainees and apprentices | Graduate trainees, apprentices under the Apprentices Act | Chapters on conduct, POSH, security, safety, leave as per their letter |
| Interns | Students on short-term internships | Conduct, POSH, security, confidentiality and safety chapters |
| Contract and agency staff | Engaged through a third-party vendor | Conduct, POSH, security, confidentiality and safety chapters; other terms are set by the vendor contract |
| Consultants and retainers | Independent professionals | Conduct, POSH, confidentiality, security and conflict of interest chapters |

Employees on a cross-border assignment remain covered by this manual, supplemented by the assignment letter, Chapters 11 and 36, and the law of the host country. Each country addendum (Chapters 38–45) adapts this manual to local law; where the addendum and a global chapter differ, the addendum applies in that country.

### 1.3 Legal framework

This manual sets global minimum standards. Each employing entity also complies with the laws of its own country, which are listed in its country addendum (Chapters 38–45). For India, where the Company's largest delivery centres are located, these policies are drafted to comply with the following laws, as amended, and the rules notified under them:

- Code on Wages, 2019
- Code on Social Security, 2020
- Industrial Relations Code, 2020
- Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020
- Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013
- Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016
- Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its rules
- Information Technology Act, 2000
- Income-tax Act and related rules on TDS and perquisites
- the Shops and Commercial Establishments Act of each State where the Company has an office
- the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) provisions, where applicable

Where a law grants an employee a benefit greater than this manual, the law prevails. Where the Company's appointment letter grants a greater benefit than this manual, the appointment letter prevails.

### 1.4 Order of precedence

If two documents conflict, they apply in this order:

1. Applicable law
2. The employee's appointment letter and any signed amendment to it
3. A signed assignment, secondment or work-mode agreement (for the matters it covers)
4. This manual
5. Departmental guidelines, standard operating procedures and FAQs

### 1.6 How to use this manual

- Read Chapters 2, 8–14 first if you want to understand how your work mode affects your day-to-day working arrangements.
- Use the HRMS for all requests (leave, work-mode changes, attendance regularisation, reimbursements). Email approvals are not valid unless the HRMS is unavailable.
- Address questions first to your manager, then to your HRBP, or to the HR helpdesk at **hr@bragslabs.com**.
- Policy forms and checklists are in Chapter 35.

### 1.7 Responsibilities

**Employees** must read this manual, acknowledge it within 15 days of joining and after each major revision, and follow it.

**Managers** must apply these policies consistently, keep their teams informed, approve requests promptly (normally within 2 working days), and consult HR before taking any disciplinary step.

**HR** owns this manual, provides guidance, keeps records, audits compliance and reports material issues to the leadership team.

**Leadership** sets the tone for ethical conduct and ensures that adequate resources exist to implement these policies.

### 1.8 Exceptions

Any exception to this manual must be approved in writing by the CHRO, and for financial exceptions also by the CFO. Exceptions are granted only for genuine business or personal hardship reasons, are recorded in the HRMS, and do not create a precedent.

### 1.9 Global standards and country rules

The Company operates through a local employing entity in each of its 8 countries. Three rules decide which rule applies to an employee:

1. **Global standard.** Chapters 1–35 apply to every employee everywhere, for example on conduct, work modes, performance, security and inclusion.
2. **Local law wins.** Where local law gives an employee more, or requires something different, local law applies. Country addenda (Chapters 38–45) set out the main local rules.
3. **Global floor.** Where local law gives less than the Company's global minimum benefits in Chapter 37, the global minimum applies.

Where a global chapter quotes Indian law, Indian statutory figures or amounts in rupees (₹), those details apply to employees of the Indian entity. Employees of other entities apply the equivalent rules and local-currency amounts in their country addendum.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Employing entity | The Company subsidiary that employs the person and pays their salary |
| Country of employment | The country of the employing entity, where the employee is normally based |
| Home country / host country | For cross-border work, the country of employment and the country where the work is temporarily done |
| Global minimum | The minimum benefit the Company provides in every country (Chapter 37) |
