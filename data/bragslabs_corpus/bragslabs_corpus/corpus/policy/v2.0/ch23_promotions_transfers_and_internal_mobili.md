## Chapter 23. Promotions, transfers and internal mobility

Promotions happen once a year based on sustained performance, readiness and an open role, and every employee — in any work mode — can apply for internal openings after 12 months in their current role.

### 23.1 Promotion criteria

An employee may be promoted to the next level when they:

- have spent at least **18 months** at the current level (12 months for L1 to L2);
- have a rating of 4 or above in the latest cycle, or 3 or above in the last two cycles;
- consistently demonstrate the competencies of the next level, as described in the career framework;
- have the support of the manager and skip-level manager;
- fill a role at the next level that exists within the approved structure (for manager levels).

### 23.2 Promotion process

1. The manager nominates the employee in the HRMS with a promotion case: evidence of impact, next-level competencies and peer or client feedback. Employees may also self-nominate.
2. Promotions to L5 and above include a panel interview or presentation.
3. A business unit promotion committee reviews all cases together, checking for consistency and for fairness across work modes, gender and locations.
4. Approved promotions are effective **1 April**; promotion increases are set as per the compensation guide, normally 10–20%.
5. Employees not promoted receive specific feedback and a development plan.

Mid-year promotions are possible only for critical roles, with CHRO approval.

### 23.3 Internal job postings (IJP)

- Open roles up to L6 are posted internally for at least **7 days** before or alongside external hiring.
- Eligibility: 12 months in the current role, rating 3 or above, not on a PIP or under disciplinary action.
- The employee informs their manager when applying; the manager cannot block the application.
- Internal candidates go through the same selection as external candidates, with feedback provided if unsuccessful.
- If selected, the release date is agreed between the two managers, normally within **30 days** (up to 60 days for client-deployed roles).
- An IJP move that changes work mode or location is treated as a work-mode change (Chapter 2) and may come with relocation support only if the Company asked for the move.

### 23.4 Company-initiated transfers

The Company may transfer an employee to another team, project, office or city for business reasons, as permitted by the appointment letter. For transfers to another city:

- at least **30 days' written notice** is given;
- the employee's personal circumstances (children's schooling, a spouse's job, caregiving, health) are considered;
- relocation support is provided under Chapter 20;
- the transfer does not reduce pay or level.

### 23.5 Employee-requested transfers

Employees may request a transfer to another office for personal reasons (marriage, a spouse's transfer, family health). Requests are considered based on available roles and business needs. Relocation costs are borne by the employee unless the CHRO approves support for hardship.

### 23.6 Rotations and stretch assignments

The Company encourages short rotations (3–6 months) and stretch projects to build skills. Rotation opportunities are posted on the internal talent marketplace and open to all work modes. Remote employees may join in-person rotations with travel support.

### 23.7 Lateral moves and career changes

Employees may move to a role in a different job family at the same level, for example from development to product management, through an IJP or a career-change programme. Where training is needed, the employee may move to the new role on a trial basis for up to 6 months and return to the previous role if it does not work out.

### 23.8 Demotion

A reduction in level happens only:

- at the employee's request (for example, to move from a management role back to an individual contributor role), with pay protected for 12 months where the CHRO approves; or
- as a disciplinary penalty after due process (Chapter 33).

A reduction in level is never used to manage performance without due process.

### 23.9 Succession planning

Business unit leaders review succession plans for critical roles every year, identifying ready-now and ready-later successors and their development needs. High-potential employees from every work mode and location are considered.
