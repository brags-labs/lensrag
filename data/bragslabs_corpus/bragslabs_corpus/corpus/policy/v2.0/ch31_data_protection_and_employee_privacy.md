## Chapter 31. Data protection and employee privacy

The Company processes employees' and candidates' personal data only for clear employment purposes, with notice and, where needed, consent, and protects it as required by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) and its rules.

**Global standard.** The principles, rights, access limits and retention rules in this chapter apply worldwide. Each entity also complies with its local privacy law — for example the EU GDPR (Germany, Netherlands, Ireland), the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, US state laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act, Singapore's PDPA, Canada's PIPEDA and provincial laws, Australia's Privacy Act 1988 and the UAE's Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (or DIFC/ADGM rules in those free zones). The **Global Data Protection Officer** is Dr. Elena Fischer, based in Berlin (dpo@bragslabs.com); local privacy contacts are listed in each addendum. References below to the DPDP Act and the Data Protection Board of India apply to India.

### 31.1 Roles

- **Data Principal:** the employee, candidate or former employee whose personal data is processed.
- **Data Fiduciary:** the Company, which decides why and how the data is processed.
- **Data Processors:** vendors that process data for the Company, such as payroll, BGV, insurance, travel and HRMS providers.
- **Grievance Officer / Data Protection Officer:** Ms. Priya Nair, Grievance Officer, India (grievance.officer@bragslabs.com), the contact for data protection queries and complaints.

### 31.2 What data is collected

| Category | Examples | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and contact | Name, date of birth, photo, address, phone, email, emergency contacts | Employment, communication, safety |
| Government identifiers | PAN, Aadhaar (masked where possible), passport, UAN | Statutory compliance, tax, visas |
| Employment | CV, education, previous employment, BGV results, appointment terms | Hiring and employment |
| Pay and finance | Salary, bank account, tax declarations, reimbursements | Payroll, tax, benefits |
| Work records | Attendance, leave, performance, training, disciplinary records | Managing employment |
| Work-mode data | Home workplace address, work-mode agreements, travel and location registrations | Work-mode administration, safety, statutory compliance |
| Health | Medical certificates, disability and accommodation information, insurance claims (through the insurer), health check-up results (with consent) | Leave, accommodation, insurance, safety |
| Family | Spouse, children and parents' details | Insurance, nominations, emergency contacts |
| System and security | Logins, access card logs, CCTV in offices, device and network logs | Security, compliance, investigations |
| Diversity data | Gender, disability (voluntary) | Equal opportunity monitoring |

The Company does not collect caste or religion except where required by law or voluntarily provided for a specific purpose, such as an ERG or a holiday choice.

### 31.3 Privacy notice and consent

At the point of collection, candidates and employees receive a privacy notice explaining what data is collected, why, how it is used and shared, how long it is kept, and their rights. Where processing relies on consent (for example, health check-up results, voluntary diversity data, photographs for marketing, or BGV of certain kinds), consent is specific and may be withdrawn at any time through the HRMS. Processing needed for employment purposes, legal obligations or safety may continue as permitted by the DPDP Act.

### 31.4 Principles

- **Purpose limitation:** data is used only for the purposes stated.
- **Data minimisation:** only the data needed is collected.
- **Accuracy:** employees keep their data up to date in the HRMS.
- **Storage limitation:** data is kept only as long as needed (31.8).
- **Security:** data is protected by access controls, encryption, logging and vendor contracts.
- **Accountability:** the Company can show how it complies.

### 31.5 Your rights

Employees and candidates may:

1. ask for a summary of their personal data processed and the processing activities;
2. ask for correction, completion or updating of inaccurate or incomplete data;
3. ask for erasure of data no longer needed for the purpose, subject to legal retention requirements;
4. withdraw consent where processing is based on consent;
5. nominate a person to exercise their rights in case of death or incapacity;
6. raise a grievance with the Grievance Officer, and, if not satisfied, complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Requests are made through the HRMS privacy request form or by email to the Grievance Officer, and are answered within **30 days**.

### 31.6 Access within the Company

| Data | Who may access |
| --- | --- |
| Personal file and pay | The employee, HR, payroll, and the manager for limited job-related information |
| Performance | The employee, manager, skip-level manager, HR, calibration panel |
| Health and accommodation | HR and medical staff only; managers are told only what adjustments are needed |
| Disciplinary and IC records | HR, investigators, IC members, Legal, decision-makers |
| Security logs | Information Security and authorised investigators |

Managers must not ask employees for, or store, personal data beyond what they need, and must not keep copies of HR records on personal or unmanaged devices.

### 31.7 Sharing with third parties

Personal data is shared only with data processors bound by contracts requiring confidentiality and security; with government authorities as required by law; with clients only where needed for a project (for example, name and BGV status for site access) and with employee notice; and with insurers, banks and benefits providers to provide benefits. The Company does not sell employee data. Cross-border transfers, including between Company entities, follow the DPDP Act, the GDPR and UK GDPR (using standard contractual clauses or other approved safeguards) and other applicable laws, under the Company's intra-group data transfer agreement.

### 31.8 Retention

| Record | Retention |
| --- | --- |
| Unsuccessful candidate data | 12 months after the hiring decision, unless the candidate consents to longer |
| Employee personnel file | Employment plus 7 years |
| Payroll and statutory records | As required by labour and tax laws (typically 8 years) |
| IC and disciplinary records | Employment plus 7 years, or longer if a legal proceeding is pending |
| CCTV footage | 60 days unless needed for an investigation |
| Security logs | 1 year, or as required by law and client contracts |

### 31.9 Monitoring and privacy

Monitoring of Company systems (Chapter 30) is limited to security, compliance and investigations. CCTV is used in office common areas and secure zones only, never in washrooms, changing rooms, wellness rooms or crèches. The Company does not monitor employees' home environments, and does not require cameras to be on outside meetings.

### 31.10 Personal data breaches

Any suspected breach of personal data is reported to Information Security within 1 hour. The Company assesses the breach and notifies the Data Protection Board and affected individuals as required by the DPDP Act and its rules.

### 31.11 Employee responsibilities

Employees who handle personal data of colleagues, candidates, clients or customers must complete data protection training, process data only as authorised, keep it secure, never share it via personal accounts, and report any breach immediately.
