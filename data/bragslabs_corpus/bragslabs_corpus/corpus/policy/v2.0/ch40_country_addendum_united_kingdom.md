## Chapter 40. Country addendum: United Kingdom

This addendum applies to employees of BragsLabs UK Ltd;

### 40.1 Key laws

Equality Act 2010 (including the duty to take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment); Working Time Regulations 1998; National Minimum Wage Act 1998; Maternity and Parental Leave Regulations; Shared Parental Leave Regulations; Pensions Act 2008 (auto-enrolment); UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018; Health and Safety at Work Act 1974; Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998; Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006 (right-to-work checks).

### 40.2 Contracts and right to work

- Every employee receives a written statement of particulars on or before the first day of work.
- Right-to-work checks are completed before employment starts and repeated for time-limited permissions.
- Sponsored workers' employment follows Home Office sponsor duties.

### 40.3 Working time

- Standard week of 37.5 hours, Monday to Friday.
- The average working week must not exceed 48 hours unless the employee signs a voluntary opt-out, which they may cancel with notice.
- Daily rest of 11 consecutive hours, weekly rest of 24 hours, and a 20-minute break for shifts over 6 hours.
- Night workers receive free health assessments.

### 40.4 Holidays (Company policy)

**25 days' paid annual leave plus the 8 UK bank holidays** (pro rata for part-time), above the statutory 5.6 weeks. Up to 5 days may be carried over to the next holiday year. Holiday pay includes regular overtime, commission and allowances as required by law.

### 40.5 Sickness

- **Company sick pay:** 10 days at full pay in the first year of service, rising to 20 days after 2 years (inclusive of SSP).
- Self-certification for up to 7 calendar days; a fit note is needed after that.

### 40.6 Family leave (Company policy)

| Leave | Statutory position | Company enhancement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maternity and adoption | 52 weeks' leave; 39 weeks' statutory pay | 14 weeks at full pay, then statutory pay |
| Paternity | 2 weeks (26 weeks' service still needed for statutory pay) | 2 weeks at full pay from day one |
| Shared parental leave | Up to 50 weeks shared | Pay enhanced to match maternity enhancement for the same period |
| Unpaid parental leave | 18 weeks per child up to age 18 | As statutory |
| Parental bereavement | 2 weeks' leave and pay | 2 weeks at full pay |
| Neonatal care leave | Up to 12 weeks | As statutory, enhanced to full pay for the first 2 weeks |
| Carer's leave | 1 week unpaid a year | 5 days paid (Chapter 37) |
| Time off for dependants | Reasonable unpaid time | Up to 2 days paid per incident |

### 40.7 Flexible working

Employees may request flexible working from their first day of employment, up to two requests in 12 months. The Company responds within 2 months, consults the employee, and refuses only for one of the statutory business reasons.

### 40.8 Pay, pension and benefits

- Paid monthly through PAYE, with income tax and National Insurance deducted.
- National Minimum Wage and National Living Wage always met; the Company aims to pay at least the voluntary Real Living Wage.
- **Pension:** auto-enrolment into the Company scheme, employer contribution of 5% of qualifying earnings, employee 3%, with salary sacrifice available.
- Private medical insurance, life assurance at 4× salary, income protection, EAP and cycle-to-work scheme.

### 40.9 Notice and termination

| Situation | Notice |
| --- | --- |
| Statutory minimum by employer | 1 week after 1 month's service, then 1 week per complete year, up to 12 weeks |
| Company contractual notice | 1 month during probation; 3 months after probation for L4+, 1 month for L1–L3 |
| By employee | As stated in the contract |

Dismissals follow a fair procedure in line with the Acas Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures. Redundancy follows consultation rules, with statutory redundancy pay for employees with 2 years' service and collective consultation where 20 or more redundancies are proposed.

### 40.10 Harassment and grievances

The Company takes reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment, including by third parties such as clients, through risk assessment, training and clear reporting routes. Complaints go to the UK Respect at Work Committee and are handled under the Acas Code. Employees may be accompanied by a colleague or trade union representative at formal grievance and disciplinary hearings. Whistleblowing is protected under the Public Interest Disclosure Act, including disclosures about sexual harassment.

### 40.11 Restrictive covenants

Post-employment restrictions are limited to what is reasonable to protect legitimate business interests, typically non-solicitation and non-dealing for 6 months, and non-competes only for senior roles where justified.
