## Chapter 34. Separation, notice period, exit and full and final settlement

Employees leaving the Company, for any reason and from any work mode, serve or are paid for the applicable notice period, complete a structured exit, and receive their full and final settlement within **2 working days** of the last working day.

### 34.1 Types of separation

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Resignation | The employee chooses to leave |
| Retirement | On reaching the retirement age of 60 years; extensions only by Board-approved re-engagement |
| End of fixed term | A fixed-term contract ends and is not renewed |
| Termination for performance | After a documented PIP (Chapter 22) |
| Dismissal for misconduct | After due process (Chapter 33) |
| Retrenchment or redundancy | Role eliminated for business reasons, following legal requirements (34.6) |
| Medical incapacity | Employee permanently unable to perform the role or any suitable alternative, after accommodation efforts |
| Death | During employment (Chapter 19) |
| Absconding | As per Chapter 33 |

### 34.2 Notice periods

| Employee category | Notice by employee | Notice by Company |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Probationers | 15 days | 15 days |
| Confirmed L1–L3 | 60 days | 60 days |
| Confirmed L4–L6 | 90 days | 90 days |
| L7 and above | 90 days | 90 days |
| Fixed-term employees | As per contract, minimum 15 days | As per contract |

The Company may pay basic pay (or wages, as the appointment letter states) in lieu of all or part of its notice. Dismissal for proven gross misconduct is without notice.

### 34.3 Resignation process

1. The employee submits the resignation in the HRMS, stating the reason (optional) and proposed last working day.
2. The manager holds a conversation within 3 working days to understand the reasons and, where appropriate, discuss retention.
3. The manager and HRBP accept the resignation in the HRMS within **5 working days**. The resignation can be withdrawn only with the Company's agreement.
4. The manager and employee agree a handover plan.
5. The last working day is confirmed in writing.

### 34.4 Notice period rules

- The employee continues to work normally in their work mode during the notice period; the Company may ask a remote or hybrid employee to work from an office for handover if reasonable.
- Notice periods may be shortened by mutual agreement. Any shortfall not waived may be recovered as basic pay for the shortfall days, or adjusted against earned leave balance with the Company's approval.
- The Company may place the employee on **garden leave** for part of the notice period, with full pay and benefits, during which they must not work for anyone else or access Company systems.
- Client-deployed employees agree their release date with the delivery manager to protect client commitments.

### 34.5 Exit process

| Step | Owner | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge transfer and handover document | Employee and manager | During notice period |
| Exit interview (in person or video; optional online survey) | HR | Last 2 weeks |
| Return of laptop, monitor, accessories, ID card, access cards, corporate card, client devices | Employee; courier pickup for remote employees | By last working day |
| Clearance from IT, Admin, Finance, Library, Security and manager | Respective teams | By last working day |
| Revocation of system access and email (out-of-office set up) | IT | End of last working day |
| Return or deletion of Company data on any personal device | Employee, confirmed in writing | By last working day |
| Signed confidentiality reminder | Employee and HR | Last working day |

### 34.6 Retrenchment and redundancy

Where roles must be reduced for business reasons, the Company will:

- consider alternatives such as redeployment, retraining, reduced hiring and voluntary separation;
- select employees on fair, objective and documented criteria, following the "last in, first out" principle within a category where required by law, unless there are recorded reasons to depart from it;
- give the notice, or pay in lieu, and the retrenchment compensation required by the Industrial Relations Code (15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service for eligible workers), plus any enhanced severance the Company decides;
- obtain government permission where required by law for the establishment's size;
- contribute to the Worker Re-skilling Fund as required by law;
- offer outplacement support and continuation of group health insurance for 3 months after exit;
- give retrenched employees preference for re-employment if the Company hires for similar roles within one year, as required by law.

The same criteria and benefits apply to employees in every work mode.

### 34.7 Full and final settlement (F&F)

The F&F is paid within **2 working days** of the last working day, as required by the Code on Wages. It includes:

- salary up to the last working day;
- earned leave encashment;
- statutory bonus and any variable pay due under the plan;
- pending reimbursements and allowances;
- notice pay where the Company ended employment without full notice;
- retrenchment compensation, where applicable;

minus permitted recoveries such as notice shortfall, advances, unreturned assets and excess leave taken.

**Gratuity** is paid within 30 days of becoming payable. **PF** transfer or withdrawal is initiated by the employee online, and the Company approves within the time required by EPFO.

If a dispute delays part of the settlement, the undisputed amount is paid on time.

### 34.8 Documents issued

- Relieving letter and experience letter (on or with the F&F, after asset clearance).
- Final payslip and F&F statement.
- Form 16 at the end of the financial year.
- Service certificate as required by law.

A relieving letter is not withheld to enforce a notice period beyond what the law and appointment letter allow.

### 34.9 Post-employment obligations

After leaving, former employees must:

- keep Company and client information confidential;
- not use the Company's intellectual property;
- not solicit Company employees or clients for 12 months, to the extent enforceable under local law;
- return any Company property later found in their possession.

Reasonable non-solicitation and confidentiality obligations continue after employment. Restrictions on working for a competitor after employment are generally not enforceable in India and are not imposed there, except as part of a lawful garden-leave period. In other countries, a post-employment non-compete is used only where local law permits it, for limited roles, and only as set out in the employment contract and country addendum.

### 34.10 Alumni

Former employees who left in good standing are invited to the Company alumni network and may be considered for rehire (Chapter 4).
