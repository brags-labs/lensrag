## Chapter 36. Global mobility and cross-border work

Every employee is employed by the Company entity in the country where they normally live and work; working in another country, for any length of time, needs approval in advance because it can trigger immigration, tax, social security and corporate-tax obligations.

### 36.3 Why approval is needed

Working in another country, even remotely and briefly, can:

- break immigration rules, if the visa or residence status does not allow work;
- create personal income tax liability in the host country, and payroll withholding obligations for the Company;
- create social security contribution obligations in the host country;
- create a "permanent establishment" for the Company, exposing it to corporate tax in that country;
- breach client contracts or data-transfer rules if client data is accessed from that country;
- trigger local employment law protections that the employee and Company did not plan for.

For these reasons, unapproved work from another country is a serious breach (Chapter 33).

### 36.4 Temporary work from another country

1. Apply in the HRMS at least **30 days** before travel, with the country, address, dates and nature of work.
2. Global Mobility checks immigration, tax, social security and permanent-establishment risk; Information Security checks access and data rules.
3. The request is approved or declined within 10 working days.
4. During the period, the employee works their home-country hours or as agreed, uses the Company VPN, and does not meet clients or sign contracts in the host country.
5. Days are counted per calendar year across all countries. Combining a workation with leave is allowed.

Countries not permitted: those under international sanctions, on the Company's restricted list, or where the employee does not have a lawful right to be present.

### 36.5 International assignments

Assignments follow Chapter 11 and the international assignment letter. In addition:

- **Compensation approach:** short-term assignees stay on home pay plus a host-country allowance; long-term assignees are paid under a home-based balance-sheet approach or a host-based package, as stated in the letter.
- **Tax equalisation:** for long-term assignments, the Company may equalise tax so the assignee pays roughly the same tax as at home. Tax advice and filing support are provided in both countries.
- **Social security:** the Company applies social security agreements (totalisation agreements) where available, such as certificates of coverage, so contributions continue in the home country where permitted.
- **Family support:** visas for spouse and children where permitted, school search and fees allowance, spouse career support, and cultural and language training.
- **Host-country law:** local minimum wages, working-time rules, leave, health and safety and anti-discrimination laws apply during the assignment where required.

### 36.6 Permanent transfers between countries

Employees may apply for open roles in another Company country through the internal job posting process. A transfer approved for the employee's own reasons is a local hire on the host entity's terms, with:

- recognition of previous Company service for seniority, long-service awards and, where local law permits, leave accrual;
- help with work permits;
- a relocation lump sum of one month's base salary (capped at USD 10,000 or equivalent) where the Company agrees to support the move.

Where the Company initiates a transfer, it provides full relocation support (Chapter 20) and a written comparison of pay and benefits before the employee decides.

### 36.7 Global compensation principles

- Pay is set by job level and the local market of the country of employment, using the same global levels (Chapter 17) and local salary bands.
- Employees doing the same job at the same level in the same location are paid within the same band, regardless of nationality, gender or work mode.
- Global variable pay and equity plans use the same performance rules everywhere, adapted to local tax and securities law.
- Benefits follow local market practice and law, and never fall below the global minimums in Chapter 37.

### 36.8 Global working across time zones

- Teams spanning several countries agree overlap hours in their team working agreement (Chapter 9), normally at least **2 hours** a day.
- Meeting times rotate so the same region is not always meeting early or late.
- Holidays differ by country; team calendars show each member's public holidays.
- Documentation and asynchronous updates are preferred to reduce late-night meetings.
- The right to disconnect (Chapter 12) applies in each person's local time, and any stricter local right-to-disconnect law applies.

### 36.9 Language

The Company's working language is English. This manual is issued in English, and translated where local law requires (for example, key documents in German for the German entity or Arabic for UAE contracts registered with authorities). Where a translation is legally binding, it prevails in that country.
