## Chapter 33. Disciplinary policy

Disciplinary action is fair, consistent and proportionate: no penalty is imposed without a written charge, a chance to respond and, for serious cases, an impartial inquiry — whether the employee works on-site, hybrid, remote or off-site.

### 33.1 Principles

- Every employee is presumed innocent until a fair process finds otherwise.
- The employee is told the allegations in writing and given a reasonable opportunity to respond.
- Decisions are based on evidence and are proportionate to the seriousness of the misconduct, the employee's record and any mitigating factors.
- Similar cases are treated similarly across teams, locations and work modes.
- The employee may appeal any penalty.
- Managers consult HR before starting any formal step.

### 33.2 Categories of misconduct

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Minor misconduct | Repeated late arrival or late logins; not keeping calendar or status updated; repeated failure to mark attendance; dress code violations; minor misuse of Company resources; unprofessional behaviour in meetings or chats; not completing mandatory training on time |
| Serious misconduct | Unauthorised absence for 3–6 days; repeated unavailability during working hours while remote; failure to meet hybrid attendance requirements after warnings; insubordination; negligence causing loss; breach of security rules without malicious intent; working from an unapproved location; unauthorised recording of meetings; breach of client site rules |
| Gross misconduct | Fraud, theft, embezzlement; falsifying records, timesheets, claims or documents; bribery; sexual harassment (on IC findings); violence or threats; deliberate data leaks or misuse of confidential information; unauthorised moonlighting or working for a competitor; unapproved work from another country; working under the influence; serious breach of client trust; retaliation against a complainant or whistleblower; concealing a security incident; repeated serious misconduct |

This list is not exhaustive.

### 33.3 Penalties

| Penalty | Typical use |
| --- | --- |
| Verbal counselling (recorded note) | First instance of minor misconduct |
| Written warning | Repeated minor misconduct or first serious misconduct; valid for 12 months |
| Final written warning | Further misconduct during a warning period or serious misconduct; valid for 12 months |
| Withholding of increment, bonus or promotion | Serious misconduct |
| Revocation of hybrid or remote working | Breaches related to work-mode rules or security |
| Removal from client deployment | Breach of client trust or client site rules |
| Recovery of loss | Where the Company suffers financial loss through the employee's misconduct or negligence, as permitted by law |
| Demotion | Serious misconduct, after inquiry |
| Suspension without pay (as penalty) | Only where permitted by applicable standing orders and law |
| Dismissal | Gross misconduct, after inquiry |

### 33.4 Disciplinary procedure

1. **Preliminary review:** the manager and HR gather facts. For minor issues, a counselling conversation may resolve the matter.
2. **Show-cause notice:** for serious or gross misconduct, HR issues a written notice describing the allegations, evidence and possible consequences. The employee replies in writing within **3 working days** (extendable on request).
3. **Decision on inquiry:** if the reply is satisfactory, the matter is closed. If not, and a major penalty may follow, a domestic inquiry is held.
4. **Domestic inquiry:** an impartial inquiry officer (not the complainant or a witness) conducts hearings. The employee may:
   - see the evidence relied on;
   - present their own evidence and witnesses;
   - cross-examine witnesses;
   - be accompanied by a co-employee.
5. **Inquiry report:** the inquiry officer reports findings on each charge.
6. **Decision:** the disciplinary authority (normally the business unit head with the CHRO) decides the penalty after considering the report and any representation from the employee.
7. **Written decision:** issued with reasons and the right of appeal.

Hearings may be held in person, or by secure video conference for remote and off-site employees, with the same safeguards. Recordings are made only with notice to all participants.

### 33.5 Suspension pending inquiry

Where misconduct is serious, or the employee's presence could affect the investigation, client relationships or the safety of others, the employee may be suspended pending inquiry, and their system access may be restricted. During suspension:

- a subsistence allowance is paid as required by law, normally 50% of wages for the first 90 days and 75% thereafter if the delay is not due to the employee;
- the employee remains available for the inquiry and may not access Company systems or premises without permission;
- the inquiry is completed as quickly as possible, normally within 90 days.

If the employee is exonerated, they receive full pay for the suspension period, less the subsistence allowance paid.

### 33.6 Absconding

If an employee is absent without information for **7 consecutive working days**:

1. HR attempts to contact the employee and their emergency contact by phone, email and message.
2. HR sends a first notice to the registered address and email, asking the employee to report or explain within 7 days.
3. If there is no response, HR sends a second notice giving a further 7 days.
4. If there is still no response, the employee's employment may be terminated after an inquiry if required, and the final settlement is processed less any notice-period recovery permitted by law.

For remote employees, HR may also request a welfare check through the emergency contact before any action.

### 33.7 Appeals

The employee may appeal in writing to the CHRO (or to the CEO if the CHRO was the disciplinary authority) within **7 working days** of the decision. The appeal authority reviews the process and the penalty and decides within 15 working days. The appeal decision is final within the Company.

### 33.8 Criminal proceedings

Where misconduct may also be a criminal offence, the Company may report it to the police and may continue its own disciplinary process independently.

### 33.9 Records

Warnings expire after 12 months for the purpose of escalation, but records are kept as per Chapter 31.
