## Chapter 3. Equal opportunity, diversity, inclusion and accessibility

The Company makes every employment decision on merit, job requirements and performance, and never on a protected characteristic.

### 3.1 Protected characteristics

No employee or candidate will be treated less favourably because of their:

- gender, gender identity or gender expression;
- sexual orientation;
- caste, tribe, religion or belief;
- place of birth, region, state of domicile or mother tongue;
- race, colour or ethnicity;
- age;
- disability, health condition or HIV status;
- marital status, pregnancy or family responsibilities;
- work mode, such as being remote rather than on-site;
- any other characteristic protected by law.

This applies to recruitment, pay, benefits, training, assignments, promotions, transfers, discipline and termination.

### 3.2 Forms of discrimination

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Treating someone less favourably because of a protected characteristic | Rejecting a qualified candidate because she is recently married |
| Indirect | Applying a rule that disadvantages a group without good reason | Requiring all team meetings at 7 pm, excluding many caregivers |
| Harassment | Unwanted conduct that violates dignity or creates a hostile environment | Repeated jokes about a colleague's accent or caste |
| Victimisation | Treating someone badly because they complained or supported a complaint | Excluding a witness from projects after an inquiry |
| Proximity bias | Favouring people who are physically present | Giving stretch assignments only to employees seen in the office |

### 3.3 Diversity and inclusion commitments

The Company commits to:

1. Publish annual diversity data by gender, disability and level, without identifying individuals.
2. Maintain balanced interview panels with at least one woman for every panel of three or more, where possible.
3. Run an annual pay-equity review and correct unexplained gaps.
4. Support employee resource groups (ERGs) for women, LGBTQIA+ employees, persons with disabilities, parents and caregivers. Each ERG has an executive sponsor and an annual budget of ₹5,00,000.
5. Provide mandatory inclusion and unconscious-bias training for all people managers.
6. Use inclusive language in job descriptions, internal communications and policies.
7. Schedule team events so remote and hybrid employees can join, and rotate in-person events across locations.

### 3.4 Persons with disabilities

In line with the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, the Company:

- has an Equal Opportunity Policy registered with the appropriate authority and published on the intranet;
- has appointed a **Liaison Officer** for persons with disabilities: Ms. Kavita Rao, Senior HR Manager (kavita.rao@bragslabs.com, +91 80 4719 2201);
- ensures that offices are accessible, including ramps, accessible washrooms, lifts with audio and Braille, and accessible parking;
- ensures that internal software, documents and trainings meet accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1 AA as a minimum);
- provides assistive technology such as screen readers, magnifiers, ergonomic aids and captioning at no cost to the employee.

### 3.5 Reasonable accommodation

An employee or candidate may request an accommodation for disability, health condition, pregnancy, religious practice or caregiving. Examples include:

- a change of work mode, such as remote or extra work-from-home days;
- flexible start and end times, or part-time work for a period;
- an ergonomic workstation, special chair or assistive software;
- a ground-floor or quieter seat;
- prayer breaks or a quiet room;
- sign language interpretation or live captions in meetings.

**Process.** The request is made to the HRBP, verbally or in writing. HR may ask for reasonable supporting information but will not ask for unnecessary medical details. HR and the manager discuss options with the employee and respond within **15 working days**. Medical information is stored separately from the personnel file and shared only on a need-to-know basis.

The Company will grant an accommodation unless it causes disproportionate hardship to the business, in which case it will offer the best available alternative.

### 3.6 Transgender and gender-diverse employees

In line with the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019:

- employees may use the name, pronouns and gender identity of their choice in Company systems, email IDs and ID cards;
- employees may use the washroom that matches their gender identity, and all-gender washrooms are available at every office;
- gender affirmation procedures are covered under the group health insurance as per the policy terms, with up to 10 days of paid leave for related medical needs;
- a **Complaint Officer** under the Act is designated: Mr. Arjun Menon, HR Business Partner (arjun.menon@bragslabs.com, +91 80 4719 2214).

### 3.7 Religious and cultural observance

Employees may use floating holidays (Chapter 16) for religious festivals not in the public holiday list. Managers should avoid scheduling important meetings on major festivals of team members where possible. A quiet or prayer room is available in offices with more than 200 employees.

### 3.8 Inclusion in hybrid and remote teams

- Meetings with any remote participant are run as fully virtual meetings: everyone has a camera and microphone, and the chat is monitored.
- Decisions made in corridor conversations are written down and shared with absent team members.
- Remote and hybrid employees have equal access to training, stretch assignments, mentors and leadership visibility.
- Managers hold regular one-to-one meetings with every team member, whatever their work mode (Chapter 22).

### 3.9 Raising a concern

Any employee who believes they have been discriminated against may raise it with their manager, HRBP, the Liaison Officer, the Complaint Officer or through the grievance and whistleblower channels in Chapter 29. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is gross misconduct.
