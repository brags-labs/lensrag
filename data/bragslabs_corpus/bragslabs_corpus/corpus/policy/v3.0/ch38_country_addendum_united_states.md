## Chapter 38. Country addendum: United States

This addendum applies to employees of BragsLabs Inc.; federal law, plus the law of the state (and sometimes city) where the employee works, governs employment, and the most favourable rule applies.

### 38.1 Key laws

Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA); Title VII of the Civil Rights Act; Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA); Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA); Equal Pay Act; Pregnant Workers Fairness Act; Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA); National Labor Relations Act (NLRA); Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA); COBRA; HIPAA; Immigration Reform and Control Act (Form I-9); WARN Act; Occupational Safety and Health Act; and state laws on wages, leave, privacy and discrimination (for example, California, New York, Washington, Texas, Massachusetts).

### 38.2 At-will employment

Unless a written contract signed by an authorised officer says otherwise, employment is **at will**: the employee or the Company may end it at any time, with or without cause or notice, as long as the reason is lawful. Nothing in this manual changes at-will status. The notice periods in Chapter 34 are requested as good practice for resignations, and the Company normally gives 2 weeks' notice or pay in lieu for terminations not caused by misconduct.

### 38.3 Hiring and eligibility

- Every new hire completes **Form I-9** within 3 business days of starting; E-Verify is used where required or adopted.
- Background checks follow the Fair Credit Reporting Act (disclosure, consent and adverse-action notices) and state and local "ban-the-box" and fair-chance laws.
- Salary history questions are not asked where state or local law prohibits them; pay ranges are included in job postings where required (for example, California, New York, Colorado, Washington).

### 38.4 Classification, hours and overtime

| Classification | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Exempt | Salaried roles meeting FLSA and state duties and salary tests; no overtime pay |
| Non-exempt | Paid overtime at 1.5× the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, and daily overtime where state law requires (for example, California over 8 hours a day) |

Non-exempt employees record all hours worked, including remote work, and receive meal and rest breaks as required by state law. Working "off the clock" is not allowed.

### 38.5 Paid time off (Company policy)

| Leave | Entitlement |
| --- | --- |
| Paid time off (vacation and personal) | 20 days a year, accruing per pay period; carry-over up to 5 days, or as state law requires; accrued unused PTO paid out at separation where state law requires |
| Paid sick leave | 10 days a year, usable for own or family illness, preventive care and safe leave; more where state or city law requires |
| Company holidays | 11 days: New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and the day after, Christmas Day, plus 1 floating holiday |

### 38.6 Parental and family leave

- **FMLA:** eligible employees (12 months' service, 1,250 hours, worksite with 50+ employees within 75 miles) may take up to **12 weeks** of job-protected leave a year for birth, adoption, a serious health condition, or caring for a family member; 26 weeks for military caregiver leave.
- **Company paid parental leave:** **16 weeks at full pay** for birth parents, and 8 weeks at full pay for non-birth parents (minimum 2 weeks under Chapter 37), running alongside FMLA and any state paid family leave benefits.
- **State programmes:** employees in states with paid family and medical leave programmes (for example, California, New York, New Jersey, Washington, Massachusetts, Colorado, Oregon) apply for state benefits; the Company tops up to full pay for the Company leave period.
- **Pregnancy accommodations** are provided under the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act and state law; lactation breaks and private space are provided under the PUMP Act.

### 38.7 Benefits and retirement

- Medical, dental and vision plans, with the Company paying 90% of the employee premium and 70% for dependants.
- Health savings account (HSA) or flexible spending accounts (FSA), as eligible.
- **401(k)** plan with a Company match of 100% of the first 5% of eligible pay, vesting immediately.
- Life and AD&D insurance at 2× base salary; short- and long-term disability insurance.
- COBRA continuation coverage after qualifying events.
- Employee Assistance Programme.

### 38.8 Payroll

Employees are paid bi-weekly (every other Friday) by direct deposit. Federal, state and local income taxes, Social Security and Medicare (FICA), and state disability or paid-leave contributions are withheld as required. Final pay is paid on the timeline set by state law (immediately or on the last day in some states, such as California).

### 38.9 Equal opportunity, harassment and complaints

The Company prohibits discrimination and harassment based on race, colour, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation and gender identity), national origin, age (40+), disability, genetic information, veteran status and any other characteristic protected by federal, state or local law. Complaints go to HR, any manager, the Respect at Work Committee or the ethics hotline. Investigations are prompt, impartial and documented. Employees may also contact the EEOC or state agencies. Sexual harassment training is provided as required by state law (for example, California, New York, Illinois).

### 38.10 Employee rights

Nothing in this manual restricts employees' rights under Section 7 of the NLRA, including the right to discuss wages and working conditions, or to report to or cooperate with government agencies. Confidentiality and non-disparagement provisions are applied consistently with these rights.

### 38.11 Restrictive covenants

Non-compete agreements are used only where state law permits, and never in states that prohibit them (for example, California, Minnesota, North Dakota, Oklahoma). Confidentiality, invention assignment (subject to state invention-assignment laws, such as California Labor Code 2870) and reasonable non-solicitation obligations apply.

### 38.12 Remote work across states

US employees may work remotely only from states where the Company is registered for payroll: Massachusetts, New York, New Jersey, Texas, California, Washington, Illinois and North Carolina. Moving to another state needs at least 30 days' approval because of payroll tax, unemployment insurance and state law obligations. Remote employees are reimbursed for necessary business expenses where state law requires (for example, California and Illinois), through the home office allowance in Chapter 14.

### 38.13 Reductions in force

Group layoffs follow the federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for covered plant closings and mass layoffs) and state "mini-WARN" laws. Severance, where offered, requires a signed release that complies with the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act for employees aged 40 or over.
