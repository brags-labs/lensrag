## Chapter 28. Prevention of sexual harassment (global standard and India POSH procedure)

The Company has zero tolerance for sexual harassment, and this policy — made under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 — protects employees in offices, at home during work, at client sites, while travelling and in every virtual workspace.

**Global standard.** The definitions, examples, zero-tolerance rule, confidentiality and protection from retaliation in this chapter apply in every country and to people of every gender. The complaint procedure in 28.4–28.10 is the statutory procedure for India. In other countries, complaints go to the local **Respect at Work Committee** (HR, Legal and a trained senior leader, with at least one woman member), which follows the same principles and timelines unless local law sets a different procedure, as described in the country addendum.

### 28.1 Scope

- **Who is protected:** every woman employee, intern, trainee, contract worker, consultant, visitor and job candidate, as provided under the Act. As a matter of Company policy, the Company also prohibits sexual harassment of employees of any gender and handles such complaints through the same Internal Committee process.
- **Who must comply:** all employees, contractors, consultants, vendors and visitors.
- **Where it applies — the "workplace" includes:**
  - Company offices, cafeterias, transport and facilities;
  - client sites, partner premises and field locations;
  - any place visited during or because of employment, including hotels, airports and conference venues;
  - Company events, offsites and parties;
  - the home workplace while working;
  - virtual workplaces: video calls, chats, email, collaboration tools, social media and messaging apps used for work or between colleagues.

### 28.2 What sexual harassment is

Sexual harassment includes any one or more of the following unwelcome acts or behaviour, directly or by implication:

- physical contact and advances;
- a demand or request for sexual favours;
- sexually coloured remarks;
- showing pornography;
- any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

It also includes situations where such conduct is connected to:

- an implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment, or threat of detrimental treatment, in employment;
- a threat about present or future employment status;
- interference with work or creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile environment;
- humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.

### 28.3 Examples in virtual and remote settings

- Sending sexually explicit messages, images, GIFs, memes or links on chat or email.
- Making sexual comments or jokes in video calls or chats, including private chats during meetings.
- Repeated unwanted requests to switch on the camera for non-work reasons, or comments about a person's appearance, clothing or home on video.
- Persistent unwanted messages, calls or video calls outside working hours.
- Taking screenshots or recordings of a colleague in a video call and sharing them in a sexual context.
- Stalking or unwanted contact on social media.
- Using virtual backgrounds, avatars or names with sexual content.

### 28.4 Internal Committee (IC)

An IC is constituted at each office or administrative unit with 10 or more employees. Remote employees are covered by the IC of their base location.

| Member | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Presiding Officer | A woman employed at a senior level |
| Employee members | At least two employees committed to the cause of women, or with experience in social work or legal knowledge |
| External member | One member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues of sexual harassment |
| Composition | At least half of the members are women |
| Term | Up to 3 years |

The names and contacts of IC members are displayed in every office, on the intranet and in the HRMS, and are shared with remote and client-deployed employees during induction. The IC can be reached by email at **ic@bragslabs.com**, through the HRMS, by phone, or in person.

### 28.5 Making a complaint

1. The aggrieved woman (or, if she cannot, a person authorised under the Act, such as a relative, friend, co-worker or legal heir) submits a written complaint to the IC within **3 months** of the incident, or of the last incident in a series. The IC may extend this by up to 3 more months for valid reasons. Complaints may be submitted by email.
2. If the complainant cannot write, IC members give reasonable help to put the complaint in writing.
3. The complaint should include details of the incident(s), dates, the respondent's name, any witnesses and any evidence such as screenshots, emails or chat records.

### 28.6 Conciliation

Before starting an inquiry, and only at the request of the complainant, the IC may try to settle the matter through conciliation. No monetary settlement may be the basis of conciliation. If a settlement is reached, the IC records it and provides copies to both parties. If the settlement terms are not complied with, the complainant may ask the IC to proceed with an inquiry.

### 28.7 Inquiry

- The IC sends a copy of the complaint to the respondent within **7 working days**; the respondent replies within **10 working days**.
- The inquiry follows the principles of natural justice: both parties are heard, may present evidence and witnesses, and receive copies of material relied on.
- Legal practitioners may not represent either party during the inquiry.
- Hearings may be held in person or by secure video conference, especially for remote and off-site parties, with confidentiality safeguards. The IC ensures the complainant is not required to face the respondent directly if this causes distress.
- The inquiry is completed within **90 days** of the complaint.
- The IC may end the inquiry or give an ex parte decision if a party fails to attend three consecutive hearings without sufficient cause, after 15 days' notice.

### 28.8 Interim relief

During the inquiry, on the complainant's written request, the IC may recommend:

- transfer of the complainant or respondent to another team, workplace or reporting line;
- leave for the complainant of up to 3 months, in addition to her normal leave;
- restraining the respondent from reporting on the complainant's work or writing her appraisal;
- changes to work mode or meeting arrangements to avoid contact.

### 28.9 Findings and action

The IC submits its report to management within **10 days** of completing the inquiry. Management acts on it within **60 days**.

If the allegation is proved, the IC may recommend action such as a written apology, warning, reprimand, censure, withholding of promotion or increment, counselling, community service, termination, and deduction from the respondent's salary as compensation to the complainant, considering the mental trauma, loss of career opportunity, medical expenses and the respondent's means.

If the allegation is not proved, no action is taken. A complaint that cannot be proved is not a false complaint. Action is taken only against a complainant who knowingly makes a false or malicious complaint, or produces forged evidence, after a separate inquiry.

### 28.10 Appeal

Either party may appeal against the IC's recommendations to the appropriate authority under the Act within **90 days** of the recommendations.

### 28.11 Confidentiality and protection from retaliation

The identity and contents of the complaint, the identities of the parties and witnesses, the inquiry proceedings and the action taken are confidential and are not published or disclosed, except as allowed by the Act. Breach of confidentiality is a disciplinary offence and may attract a penalty. Retaliation against a complainant, witness or IC member is gross misconduct.

### 28.12 Third-party harassment

If the respondent is a client employee, vendor or visitor, the IC still receives the complaint, and the Company takes all steps within its power, including informing the third party's organisation, removing the employee from the client site, and supporting the employee in filing a complaint with the third party's committee or the police.

### 28.13 Company duties

The Company will:

- provide a safe working environment;
- display the consequences of sexual harassment and the IC details;
- run POSH awareness for all employees at induction and annually, and IC training for members;
- provide facilities for the IC to conduct inquiries, including secure virtual hearing facilities;
- help the complainant file a police complaint if she chooses to, where the conduct is an offence under law;
- treat sexual harassment as misconduct under the disciplinary rules;
- include in its annual report the number of complaints filed and disposed of, and file the IC's annual report with the District Officer as required.

### 28.14 Responsibilities of managers

Managers who become aware of possible sexual harassment must not investigate it themselves. They must support the affected person, inform them of the IC process, and inform HR or the IC with the person's knowledge.
