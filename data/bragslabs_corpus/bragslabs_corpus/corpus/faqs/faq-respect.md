# Employee FAQ: Harassment, grievances and whistleblowing

*Maintained by the HR Policy Office. Last reviewed 20 October 2026. If this FAQ and the Global HR Policy Manual differ, the manual and any later HR circular prevail.*

## Q1. What does the policy say about scope?

- **Who is protected:** every woman employee, intern, trainee, contract worker, consultant, visitor and job candidate, as provided under the Act. As a matter of Company policy, BragsLabs also prohibits sexual harassment of employees of any gender and handles such complaints through the same Internal Committee process.
- **Who must comply:** all employees, contractors, consultants, vendors and visitors.
- **Where it applies — the "workplace" includes:**
  - Company offices, cafeterias, transport and facilities;
  - client sites, partner premises and field locations;
  - any place visited during or because of employment, including hotels, airports and conference venues;
  - Company events, offsites and parties;
  - the home workplace while working;
  - virtual workplaces: video calls, chats, email, collaboration tools, social media and messaging apps used for work or between colleagues.

*Policy reference: Section 28.1.*

## Q2. Where can I find guidance on what sexual harassment is?

Sexual harassment includes any one or more of the following unwelcome acts or behaviour, directly or by implication:

- physical contact and advances;
- a demand or request for sexual favours;
- sexually coloured remarks;
- showing pornography;
- any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

It also includes situations where such conduct is connected to:

- an implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment, or threat of detrimental treatment, in employment;
- a threat about present or future employment status;
- interference with work or creating an intimidating, offensive or hostile environment;
- humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.

*Policy reference: Section 28.2.*

## Q3. How does examples in virtual and remote settings work at BragsLabs?

- Sending sexually explicit messages, images, GIFs, memes or links on chat or email.
- Making sexual comments or jokes in video calls or chats, including private chats during meetings.
- Repeated unwanted requests to switch on the camera for non-work reasons, or comments about a person's appearance, clothing or home on video.
- Persistent unwanted messages, calls or video calls outside working hours.
- Taking screenshots or recordings of a colleague in a video call and sharing them in a sexual context.
- Stalking or unwanted contact on social media.
- Using virtual backgrounds, avatars or names with sexual content.

*Policy reference: Section 28.3.*

## Q4. Can you explain the rules on internal Committee (IC)?

An IC is constituted at each office or administrative unit with 10 or more employees. Remote employees are covered by the IC of their base location.

| Member | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Presiding Officer | A woman employed at a senior level |
| Employee members | At least two employees committed to the cause of women, or with experience in social work or legal knowledge |
| External member | One member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues of sexual harassment |
| Composition | At least half of the members are women |
| Term | Up to 3 years |

The names and contacts of IC members are displayed in every office, on the intranet and in the HRMS, and are shared with remote and client-deployed employees during induction. The IC can be reached by email at **ic@bragslabs.com**, through the HRMS, by phone, or in person.

*Policy reference: Section 28.4.*

## Q5. Where can I find guidance on making a complaint?

1. The aggrieved woman (or, if she cannot, a person authorised under the Act, such as a relative, friend, co-worker or legal heir) submits a written complaint to the IC within **3 months** of the incident, or of the last incident in a series. The IC may extend this by up to 3 more months for valid reasons. Complaints may be submitted by email.
2. If the complainant cannot write, IC members give reasonable help to put the complaint in writing.
3. The complaint should include details of the incident(s), dates, the respondent's name, any witnesses and any evidence such as screenshots, emails or chat records.

*Policy reference: Section 28.5.*

## Q6. What does the policy say about conciliation?

Before starting an inquiry, and only at the request of the complainant, the IC may try to settle the matter through conciliation. No monetary settlement may be the basis of conciliation. If a settlement is reached, the IC records it and provides copies to both parties. If the settlement terms are not complied with, the complainant may ask the IC to proceed with an inquiry.

*Policy reference: Section 28.6.*

## Q7. What does the policy say about inquiry?

- The IC sends a copy of the complaint to the respondent within **7 working days**; the respondent replies within **10 working days**.
- The inquiry follows the principles of natural justice: both parties are heard, may present evidence and witnesses, and receive copies of material relied on.
- Legal practitioners may not represent either party during the inquiry.
- Hearings may be held in person or by secure video conference, especially for remote and off-site parties, with confidentiality safeguards. The IC ensures the complainant is not required to face the respondent directly if this causes distress.
- The inquiry is completed within **90 days** of the complaint.
- The IC may end the inquiry or give an ex parte decision if a party fails to attend three consecutive hearings without sufficient cause, after 15 days' notice.

*Policy reference: Section 28.7.*

## Q8. Where can I find guidance on interim relief?

During the inquiry, on the complainant's written request, the IC may recommend:

- transfer of the complainant or respondent to another team, workplace or reporting line;
- leave for the complainant of up to 3 months, in addition to her normal leave;
- restraining the respondent from reporting on the complainant's work or writing her appraisal;
- changes to work mode or meeting arrangements to avoid contact.

*Policy reference: Section 28.8.*

## Q9. How does findings and action work at BragsLabs?

The IC submits its report to management within **10 days** of completing the inquiry. Management acts on it within **60 days**.

If the allegation is proved, the IC may recommend action such as a written apology, warning, reprimand, censure, withholding of promotion or increment, counselling, community service, termination, and deduction from the respondent's salary as compensation to the complainant, considering the mental trauma, loss of career opportunity, medical expenses and the respondent's means.

If the allegation is not proved, no action is taken. A complaint that cannot be proved is not a false complaint. Action is taken only against a complainant who knowingly makes a false or malicious complaint, or produces forged evidence, after a separate inquiry.

*Policy reference: Section 28.9.*

## Q10. How does appeal work at BragsLabs?

Either party may appeal against the IC's recommendations to the appropriate authority under the Act within **90 days** of the recommendations.

*Policy reference: Section 28.10.*

## Q11. What should I know about confidentiality and protection from retaliation?

The identity and contents of the complaint, the identities of the parties and witnesses, the inquiry proceedings and the action taken are confidential and are not published or disclosed, except as allowed by the Act. Breach of confidentiality is a disciplinary offence and may attract a penalty. Retaliation against a complainant, witness or IC member is gross misconduct.

*Policy reference: Section 28.11.*

## Q12. How does third-party harassment work at BragsLabs?

If the respondent is a client employee, vendor or visitor, the IC still receives the complaint, and BragsLabs takes all steps within its power, including informing the third party's organisation, removing the employee from the client site, and supporting the employee in filing a complaint with the third party's committee or the police.

*Policy reference: Section 28.12.*

## Q13. Where can I find guidance on company duties?

BragsLabs will:

- provide a safe working environment;
- display the consequences of sexual harassment and the IC details;
- run POSH awareness for all employees at induction and annually, and IC training for members;
- provide facilities for the IC to conduct inquiries, including secure virtual hearing facilities;
- help the complainant file a police complaint if she chooses to, where the conduct is an offence under law;
- treat sexual harassment as misconduct under the disciplinary rules;
- include in its annual report the number of complaints filed and disposed of, and file the IC's annual report with the District Officer as required.

*Policy reference: Section 28.13.*

## Q14. What does the policy say about responsibilities of managers?

Managers who become aware of possible sexual harassment must not investigate it themselves. They must support the affected person, inform them of the IC process, and inform HR or the IC with the person's knowledge.

*Policy reference: Section 28.14.*

## Q15. Can you explain the rules on which channel to use?

| Concern | Channel |
| --- | --- |
| Sexual harassment | Internal Committee (Chapter 28) |
| Pay, leave, workload, work mode, assignments, treatment by manager or colleagues, discrimination | Grievance redressal (29.2–29.5) |
| Fraud, bribery, data misuse, falsified records, safety risks, legal violations, serious policy breaches | Whistleblower channel (29.6–29.10) |
| Disability-related issues | Liaison Officer and grievance redressal |
| Data protection concerns about your own data | Grievance Officer for data protection (Chapter 31) |
| Immediate danger to life or safety | Emergency helpline, then manager and HR (Chapter 32) |

*Policy reference: Section 29.1.*

## Q16. Can you explain the rules on grievance redressal: principles?

- Grievances are resolved at the lowest appropriate level, quickly and fairly.
- Both sides are heard before any decision.
- Information is shared only with those who need it to resolve the grievance.
- Raising a genuine grievance never leads to adverse treatment.

*Policy reference: Section 29.2.*

## Q17. How does grievance procedure work at BragsLabs?

| Stage | Raised with | Response time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Informal | Immediate manager (or skip-level manager if the grievance is about the manager) | 7 working days |
| 2. Formal | HRBP, in writing through the HRMS grievance form | 7 working days for acknowledgement and meeting; 15 working days for resolution |
| 3. Committee | Grievance Redressal Committee | 30 days from the reference to the Committee |
| 4. Appeal | CHRO (for decisions of the Committee where permitted by law) | 15 working days |

Grievance meetings may be held in person or by video. You may be accompanied by a colleague of their choice.

*Policy reference: Section 29.3.*

## Q18. Where can I find guidance on grievance Redressal Committee?

In line with the Industrial Relations Code, each establishment with 20 or more workers has a Grievance Redressal Committee:

- with equal numbers of members from the employer and the workers, up to a maximum of 10;
- with a chairperson selected alternately from the employer side and the worker side each year;
- with adequate representation of women, in proportion to the women workers in the establishment;
- which completes its proceedings within 30 days of receiving an application.

A worker who is not satisfied with the Committee's decision may pursue the remedies available under the Industrial Relations Code.

*Policy reference: Section 29.4.*

## Q19. How does group grievances work at BragsLabs?

Where several employees share a grievance (for example, about a team's working hours or transport), they may raise it together through a representative. HR responds to the group and communicates the outcome to all concerned.

*Policy reference: Section 29.5.*

## Q20. How does whistleblower policy: purpose work at BragsLabs?

BragsLabs encourages employees, contractors, vendors, clients and others to report suspected wrongdoing ("protected disclosures") so it can be investigated and stopped. The policy also serves as the vigil mechanism required under the Companies Act, 2013, where applicable.

*Policy reference: Section 29.6.*

## Q21. What does the policy say about what to report?

- Fraud, theft, embezzlement or misuse of Company funds or assets.
- Bribery, kickbacks or improper payments.
- Falsification of financial records, timesheets, billing or client reports.
- Breaches of data protection, information security or client confidentiality.
- Violation of law, including labour, tax, environmental and competition law.
- Unsafe practices that endanger health or safety.
- Discrimination, harassment or abuse of authority not covered by the IC process.
- Retaliation against anyone who raised a concern.
- Deliberate concealment of any of the above.

The whistleblower channel is not for routine personal grievances, which go through 29.3.

*Policy reference: Section 29.7.*

## Q22. Can you explain the rules on how to report?

- Email the Ethics Committee at **ethics@bragslabs.com**.
- Use the 24x7 independent ethics hotline (phone and web) at **+1 800 555 0199 (US and Canada), 000 800 919 1234 (India) or ethicsline.bragslabs.com**, which allows anonymous reports.
- Write to the Chief Ethics Officer by post at BragsLabs, Ethics Office, 200 Summit Avenue, Suite 1400, Boston, MA 02110, USA.
- In exceptional cases, or if the concern involves senior management, write directly to the Chairperson of the Audit Committee at audit.chair@bragslabs.com.

Reports should include as much detail as possible: what happened, when, where, who was involved, and any evidence. Anonymous reports are investigated if they contain enough information to act on.

*Policy reference: Section 29.8.*

## Q23. Can you explain the rules on investigation?

1. The Ethics Committee acknowledges the report within **3 working days** (where the reporter can be contacted).
2. A preliminary review within **15 days** decides whether a full investigation is needed.
3. Investigations are led by an independent investigator from Internal Audit, Legal, HR or an external firm, depending on the matter, and are completed within **60 days** where possible.
4. Those being investigated are informed of the allegations and given a chance to respond, unless this would prejudice the investigation.
5. The Committee decides on actions and informs the reporter of the outcome, as far as confidentiality permits.
6. Serious matters and summaries are reported to the Audit Committee every quarter.

*Policy reference: Section 29.9.*

## Q24. How does protection of whistleblowers work at BragsLabs?

- The identity of the whistleblower is kept confidential and disclosed only where required by law or with their consent.
- No one may retaliate against a whistleblower, through dismissal, demotion, transfer, denial of increments or promotions, harassment or any other adverse action. Retaliation is gross misconduct.
- A whistleblower who reports in good faith is protected even if the concern turns out to be unfounded.
- Knowingly making false or malicious allegations is misconduct.
- A whistleblower who was involved in the wrongdoing is not protected from action for their own part, but voluntary reporting is considered as a mitigating factor.

*Policy reference: Section 29.10.*

## Q25. Can you explain the rules on records?

All grievances and disclosures, investigation reports and decisions are recorded confidentially and kept for at least 8 years.

*Policy reference: Section 29.11.*
