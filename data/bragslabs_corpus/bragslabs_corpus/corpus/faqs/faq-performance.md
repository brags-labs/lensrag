# Employee FAQ: Performance and promotions

*Maintained by the HR Policy Office. Last reviewed 20 October 2026. If this FAQ and the Global HR Policy Manual differ, the manual and any later HR circular prevail.*

## Q1. What does the policy say about the annual cycle?

| When | Activity | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April | Goal setting: 4–6 goals, aligned with team and Company objectives, recorded in the HRMS | Employee and manager |
| Every 2 weeks | One-to-one meetings with feedback and coaching | Manager |
| Quarterly | Goal progress check and update; client feedback for deployed employees | Employee and manager |
| October | Mid-year review: written feedback and development plan; no rating | Manager |
| March | Self-assessment, peer feedback (3–5 reviewers), manager assessment | Employee, peers, manager |
| March–April | Calibration by a cross-team panel | Business unit leadership and HR |
| April–May | Rating conversation and written review shared | Manager |
| June | Increment, promotion and variable pay letters | HR |

*Policy reference: Section 22.1.*

## Q2. What does the policy say about goal setting?

Goals are SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. Each goal has a weight, and at least one goal relates to learning or team contribution. For client-deployed employees, goals reflect the client engagement and the client's feedback is collected.

*Policy reference: Section 22.2.*

## Q3. Can you explain the rules on what is assessed?

| Dimension | Weight | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Results | 70% | Delivery of goals, quality, client satisfaction, business impact |
| Behaviours | 30% | Collaboration, ownership, integrity, communication, learning, inclusion |

Activity measures such as hours seen online, office attendance beyond policy requirements or speed of chat replies are not used to rate performance. Compliance with work-mode rules (for example, the hybrid office-day requirement) is a conduct expectation and may be noted in the behaviours section.

*Policy reference: Section 22.3.*

## Q4. How does rating scale work at BragsLabs?

| Rating | Label | Description | Indicative distribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Exceptional | Consistently far exceeds goals; role model | 5–10% |
| 4 | Exceeds expectations | Exceeds most goals; strong contribution | 20–25% |
| 3 | Meets expectations | Achieves goals; solid, reliable performance | 50–60% |
| 2 | Partly meets | Meets some goals; improvement needed | 5–10% |
| 1 | Does not meet | Significantly below expectations | 0–5% |

The distribution is a guide for calibration, not a forced quota.

*Policy reference: Section 22.4.*

## Q5. What does the policy say about fair assessment across work modes?

To prevent proximity bias:

- Calibration panels review rating patterns by work mode, gender and location, and query any gap.
- Managers base assessments on documented evidence (deliverables, metrics, feedback), not on impressions.
- Remote and off-site you must receive equal access to visible assignments and to the manager's time.
- Client feedback for off-site employees is collected in writing through the delivery manager.
- Employees on maternity, long medical or other protected leave are rated on the period worked and are not penalised for the leave; where the period worked is under 3 months, they receive at least a "meets expectations" rating for pay purposes.

*Policy reference: Section 22.5.*

## Q6. Where can I find guidance on one-to-ones and continuous feedback?

- Managers hold one-to-ones at least every two weeks; video with cameras on is recommended for remote employees.
- Feedback is specific, timely and balanced; recognition is given publicly where appropriate, corrective feedback privately.
- Employees can request feedback from anyone through the HRMS at any time.

*Policy reference: Section 22.6.*

## Q7. What does the policy say about managing performance in distributed teams?

Managers of hybrid, remote and off-site teams should:

1. Make expectations explicit and written: outcomes, deadlines, quality bars and communication norms.
2. Use shared trackers so progress is visible without micro-management.
3. Hold short team stand-ups and a weekly team review.
4. Check in on workload and wellbeing, not only on tasks.
5. Document key conversations and agreements in writing.
6. Meet each team member in person at least once or twice a year where possible.

*Policy reference: Section 22.7.*

## Q8. How does performance improvement plan (PIP) work at BragsLabs?

A PIP is used when an employee is rated 1 or 2, or when there are documented performance concerns during the year.

1. The manager raises the concern informally first and gives at least 4 weeks to improve.
2. If performance does not improve, the manager and HRBP prepare a written PIP of **60–90 days**, with specific, measurable goals, support and resources.
3. Progress reviews are held every two weeks and documented.
4. At the end of the PIP, the outcome is: successful completion, extension (once, up to 30 days), role change, or separation with notice.
5. HR attends the start and closing meetings. You may bring a colleague as a support person.

A PIP is a support process and is not a disciplinary penalty. Employees on PIP are not eligible for work-mode changes or internal transfers until it is completed.

*Policy reference: Section 22.8.*

## Q9. What should I know about disagreeing with a rating?

You who disagrees with their rating may raise a review request in the HRMS within **15 days** of the rating conversation. The skip-level manager and HRBP review the evidence and respond within 21 days. Their decision is final for that cycle.

*Policy reference: Section 22.9.*

## Q10. What should I know about probationers, new joiners and leavers?

Employees who joined after 1 January are not rated in that cycle but receive feedback. Employees serving notice are not rated.

*Policy reference: Section 22.10.*

## Q11. Can you explain the rules on promotion criteria?

You may be promoted to the next level when they:

- have spent at least **18 months** at the current level (12 months for L1 to L2);
- have a rating of 4 or above in the latest cycle, or 3 or above in the last two cycles;
- consistently demonstrate the competencies of the next level, as described in the career framework;
- have the support of the manager and skip-level manager;
- fill a role at the next level that exists within the approved structure (for manager levels).

*Policy reference: Section 23.1.*

## Q12. Can you explain the rules on promotion process?

1. The manager nominates the employee in the HRMS with a promotion case: evidence of impact, next-level competencies and peer or client feedback. You may also self-nominate.
2. Promotions to L5 and above include a panel interview or presentation.
3. A business unit promotion committee reviews all cases together, checking for consistency and for fairness across work modes, gender and locations.
4. Approved promotions are effective **1 April**; promotion increases are set as per the compensation guide, normally 10–20%.
5. Employees not promoted receive specific feedback and a development plan.

Mid-year promotions are possible only for critical roles, with CHRO approval.

*Policy reference: Section 23.2.*

## Q13. What does the policy say about internal job postings (IJP)?

- Open roles up to L6 are posted internally for at least **7 days** before or alongside external hiring.
- Eligibility: 12 months in the current role, rating 3 or above, not on a PIP or under disciplinary action.
- You informs their manager when applying; the manager cannot block the application.
- Internal candidates go through the same selection as external candidates, with feedback provided if unsuccessful.
- If selected, the release date is agreed between the two managers, normally within **30 days** (up to 60 days for client-deployed roles).
- An IJP move that changes work mode or location is treated as a work-mode change (Chapter 2) and may come with relocation support only if BragsLabs asked for the move.

*Policy reference: Section 23.3.*

## Q14. What should I know about company-initiated transfers?

BragsLabs may transfer an employee to another team, project, office or city for business reasons, as permitted by the appointment letter. For transfers to another city:

- at least **30 days' written notice** is given;
- your personal circumstances (children's schooling, a spouse's job, caregiving, health) are considered;
- relocation support is provided under Chapter 20;
- the transfer does not reduce pay or level.

*Policy reference: Section 23.4.*

## Q15. How does employee-requested transfers work at BragsLabs?

You may request a transfer to another office for personal reasons (marriage, a spouse's transfer, family health). Requests are considered based on available roles and business needs. Relocation costs are borne by the employee unless the CHRO approves support for hardship.

*Policy reference: Section 23.5.*

## Q16. What does the policy say about rotations and stretch assignments?

BragsLabs encourages short rotations (3–6 months) and stretch projects to build skills. Rotation opportunities are posted on the internal talent marketplace and open to all work modes. Remote you may join in-person rotations with travel support.

*Policy reference: Section 23.6.*

## Q17. Where can I find guidance on lateral moves and career changes?

You may move to a role in a different job family at the same level, for example from development to product management, through an IJP or a career-change programme. Where training is needed, the employee may move to the new role on a trial basis for up to 6 months and return to the previous role if it does not work out.

*Policy reference: Section 23.7.*

## Q18. How does demotion work at BragsLabs?

A reduction in level happens only:

- at your request (for example, to move from a management role back to an individual contributor role), with pay protected for 12 months where the CHRO approves; or
- as a disciplinary penalty after due process (Chapter 33).

A reduction in level is never used to manage performance without due process.

*Policy reference: Section 23.8.*

## Q19. What should I know about succession planning?

Business unit leaders review succession plans for critical roles every year, identifying ready-now and ready-later successors and their development needs. High-potential employees from every work mode and location are considered.

*Policy reference: Section 23.9.*
