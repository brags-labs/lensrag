# Employee FAQ: working at BragsLabs in Canada

*For employees of BragsLabs Canada Inc. Last reviewed 29 September 2026. The Canada addendum (Chapter 46 of manual version 3.0) prevails if this FAQ differs.*

## Q10. What does the policy say about key laws?

**Ontario:** Employment Standards Act, 2000 (ESA); Human Rights Code; Occupational Health and Safety Act (including workplace harassment and violence programmes); Pay Equity Act; Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act; Working for Workers Acts. **Federal:** Canada Pension Plan; Employment Insurance Act; Income Tax Act; PIPEDA. **Other provinces:** Quebec's Act respecting labour standards, Charter of the French Language and Law 25 (privacy); British Columbia's Employment Standards Act and Pay Transparency Act; Alberta's Employment Standards Code.

## Q11. How does hiring work at BragsLabs?

- Publicly advertised job postings in Ontario include the expected pay range, state whether a vacancy exists, disclose the use of AI to screen or assess applicants, and do not require Canadian work experience, as required for employers with 25 or more employees.
- Interviewed candidates are told the outcome within the time required by law.
- Background checks and reference checks follow human rights and privacy law.

## Q12. What should I know about working hours and overtime?

- Standard week of 37.5 hours.
- Ontario limits are 8 hours a day (or the regular workday if longer) and 48 hours a week, unless the employee agrees in writing to more within legal limits.
- Overtime is paid at 1.5× the regular rate after **44 hours a week** in Ontario (different thresholds apply in other provinces, such as 40 hours a week and 8 hours a day in British Columbia and Alberta). Managers and certain IT professionals may be exempt as provided by provincial law.
- 11 consecutive hours off each day; 30-minute meal break after 5 hours.

## Q13. How does right to disconnect and electronic monitoring work at BragsLabs?

In line with Ontario law, BragsLabs has a written **policy on disconnecting from work** (Chapter 12) and a written **electronic monitoring policy** (Chapters 30 and 31) explaining whether, how and why employees are electronically monitored. Both are given to employees on hiring and when changed.

## Q15. What does the policy say about sick and personal leave?

- Ontario ESA provides 3 unpaid sick days, 3 family responsibility days and 2 bereavement days a year; federally regulated employers and some provinces (for example, British Columbia, with 5 paid days) require paid sick days.
- **Company policy:** 10 paid sick and personal days a year, plus 5 days' paid bereavement leave (Chapter 37).
- Other job-protected leaves are provided as required, including family caregiver, critical illness, organ donor, reservist, and domestic or sexual violence leave (10 days, of which the first 5 are paid in Ontario, plus up to 15 weeks).

## Q17. Where can I find guidance on pay and benefits?

- Paid bi-weekly by direct deposit; income tax, CPP (or QPP in Quebec) and EI premiums deducted.
- Provincial minimum wages always met.
- Extended health and dental benefits, vision care, life and AD&D insurance at 2× salary, short- and long-term disability, EAP.
- Group RRSP with a Company match of up to 5% of base salary, or a deferred profit-sharing plan.

## Q18. Where can I find guidance on termination?

- **Ontario ESA notice:** 1 week per year of service, up to 8 weeks (or pay in lieu), with benefits continued during the notice period.
- **Ontario ESA severance pay:** 1 week's pay per year of service (up to 26 weeks) for employees with 5 or more years' service where the employer's global payroll is at least $2.5 million.
- **Common law:** unless an enforceable written termination clause limits entitlements to the statutory minimums, you may be entitled to "reasonable notice" at common law, which can be considerably longer. Employment contracts are drafted carefully for this reason.
- Mass terminations (50 or more employees in 4 weeks in Ontario) need longer notice and notification to the Ministry.
- Non-compete agreements are prohibited in Ontario except for executives and on the sale of a business; confidentiality and non-solicitation obligations apply.

## Q19. How does harassment, human rights and accessibility work at BragsLabs?

BragsLabs maintains a workplace harassment and violence policy and programme (including sexual harassment) in each province, investigates every complaint, and reviews the programme annually. Human rights codes prohibit discrimination and require accommodation to the point of undue hardship. Quebec employees are protected against psychological harassment under Quebec law. BragsLabs meets AODA accessibility standards in Ontario.
